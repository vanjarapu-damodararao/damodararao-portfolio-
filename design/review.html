Phase 01

Creative direction: kept, sharpened.

The idea in one line

A website is drawn like a building.

Architects draw a building on paper before it's built. You build websites in layers the same way. So the whole portfolio looks like a set of technical drawings: paper, ink lines, measurements, and one orange "marker" colour.

Why it works for you

It's about how you think.

Most developer portfolios are dark, neon and full of cards. A paper-and-ink drawing set looks careful and planned. That's exactly what a client or recruiter wants to see in someone who builds their website.

What I changed

Clearer words, real you.

I kept the look. I changed the meaning where it was too "architect-y": plain web words instead of building jargon in the hero, your real photo, and wording that matches your real experience (React, Laravel, 2+ years).

Verdict

Keep the concept. Fix the meaning: "From blueprint to browser."

The exploded-layers idea is strong, so I'm not replacing it with a new object. The problem is that the finished thing looks like a building, not a website. The fix: each floor is shaped like a web page and shows what that layer really does. When you scroll, the floors close together, a browser window lands on top, and the page goes live. In 3 seconds a visitor understands: this person takes a website from plan to launch.

Storyboard: what the visitor sees from first load to the end of the hero scroll. These are design drawings. I'll build it in 3D with Three.js after you approve.

Phase 03

Design system: the rules every page follows.

Paper#ECE7DC · backgrounds. A warm off-white that's softer on the eyes than pure white.
Ink#16181D · text and lines. Almost black, so it doesn't look harsh.
Graphite#5C6068 · secondary text. Dark enough to be readable (passes accessibility).
Survey orange#FF4F1A · only for markers, active states and "live". If it's used everywhere, it stops meaning anything.

How much of each colour: 70% paper · 20% ink · 7% blueprint navy (only the Experiments section) · 3% orange. Keeping orange rare is what makes the design feel premium.

Archivo · headings and UI

Build

A wide, strong, slightly technical typeface, like the lettering on engineering drawings. It's used big and in capitals for headings, and normal for body text.

Instrument Serif italic · editorial accents

beyond the ordinary.

A thin, elegant italic. Used for only a few words per heading, like a designer's handwritten note on a drawing. That contrast is what makes it feel "designed".

Every gap uses one of these numbers. Sections are separated by 160px on desktop and 80px on phones. Lots of empty paper makes a page look expensive.

  • Grid 12 columns on desktop, 1 on phones. Content max 1440px wide, side margins 16–56px.
  • Lines 1px hairlines for structure, 1.5px for emphasis. No shadows and no rounded cards, because drawings don't have them.
  • Breakpoints Desktop over 1100 · Tablet 861–1100 · Phone up to 860 (small phones up to 420).
  • Motion Things slide in once as you scroll, and lines "draw" themselves. Nothing loops forever except the hero.
  • Easing Fast start, gentle stop (like a pen stroke). Animations last 0.9–1.4 seconds.
  • Accessibility If a visitor turns on "reduce motion", every animation switches off. The 3D has a static drawing fallback.

Phase 05

Your portrait: printed into the drawing.

What I did, and why

Your face is 100% your real photo. Nothing is generated. I printed it in "ink": black-and-white with a little extra contrast, blended into the paper. The white studio background disappears into the page, so it stops looking like an ID photo and starts looking like a plate in a printed book. Orange corner marks frame it like a drawing. When a visitor hovers over it, a little colour comes back.

Why black-and-white? Your blue checked shirt fights the paper-and-ink colours. In grayscale it reads as a subtle texture instead.

Next step (optional): an art-directed portrait. For a charcoal or graphite shirt and natural side light, we need an AI image tool that keeps your face. I can do this with the image generator connected to this app, but it means uploading your photo to that third-party service, so I'll only do it if you say yes. The best option of all is a 10-minute photo with any phone: a charcoal/grey/black plain shirt, standing next to a window (side light), in front of a plain light wall. That always looks more real than AI.

Phase 06

  • Pass

  • Fix

A recruiter who reads both will notice. I recommend "Creative Web Developer · React & Laravel". It keeps the creative positioning and adds the skills they search for.

  • Later
